**Answer Scale Reference (Updated)**

v2 switches from a 5-point to a 6-point behavioral scale with no middle option. This forces commitment (no 'avoid the question' middle), uses behavioral language instead of agreement language (more natural), and resolves your concern about the agree/strongly-agree ambiguity.

| 6 | Sounds exactly like me |
| :---- | :---- |
| **5** | Sounds like me |
| **4** | Sounds a bit like me |
| **3** | Doesn't quite sound like me |
| **2** | Doesn't sound like me |
| **1** | Sounds nothing like me |

*Forced-choice questions present two scenarios (A or B). Both options are framed positively to avoid 'right answer' bias. We use these for \~12 questions across the pool — they feel more natural and produce better data on dimensions where direct self-report is unreliable (introversion/extraversion, family vs personal motivation, entrepreneurial vs employed preference).*

**Report Handling — Resilience & Workplace Mindset**

Critical: how the report handles these scores determines whether this framework helps or harms the user. The construct is sensitive, especially for younger users who may already feel judged about their generation's relationship to work.

**For LOW Workplace Resilience scores:**

*NEVER frame as 'you can't handle work.' Frame as: 'You have strong instincts about environments that don't suit you. The careers we've recommended take this seriously — they tend to have higher autonomy, more supportive feedback cultures, and clearer expectations. **Translation Guidance for Bilingual Build**

The Arabic version is the make-or-break for cultural fit. A few principles before your translator starts:

* Use Khaleeji Arabic register, NOT formal Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). MSA reads as a school exam to this audience.  
* Code-switching is fine — keep modern career terms in English where they're more natural ('startup', 'CEO', 'remote work', 'side hustle'). Don't force Arabic translations that sound stilted.  
* Test the Arabic with 8–12 actual users from the target demographic before launch. Listen for any question that makes them pause or ask 'what does this mean?' — those are the ones to rewrite.  
* Special care on Values questions, especially Family (Q31, Q32), Reputation (Q39, Q40), and National Contribution (Q37, Q38). These are the most culturally loaded.  
* Reverse-scored questions (Q21, Q22) — make sure the Arabic doesn't accidentally make 'agree' feel negative.  
* Forced-choice scenario questions: both options must feel equally aspirational in Arabic. Watch for any subtle moralization.
